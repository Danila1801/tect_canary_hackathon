# Legal Watch alert LW-2026-041: EU Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970

The transposition deadline of the EU Pay Transparency Directive passed on 7 June 2026. The obligations below
must be reflected in recruitment processes, contract templates and employee self-service content.

## Recruitment
- Employers may not ask job applicants about their pay history in current or previous employment (Art. 5(2)).
- Applicants must receive information about the initial pay or its range, based on objective, gender-neutral
  criteria, before the job interview (Art. 5(1)).

## During employment
- Contractual terms that prevent workers from disclosing their own pay are not allowed (Art. 7(5)).
- Workers may request information on their individual pay level and on the average pay levels, broken down by
  sex, for categories of workers doing the same work or work of equal value (Art. 7(1)).
- The employer must answer within a reasonable period, and at the latest within two months (Art. 7(4)).
- Employers must inform all workers of this right every year (Art. 7(3)).
- Individual pay of a named colleague is not part of this right.

## Reporting
- 250 or more workers: first gender pay gap report by 7 June 2027, then every year.
- 150 to 249 workers: first report by 7 June 2027, then every three years.
- 100 to 149 workers: first report by 7 June 2031, then every three years.
- A joint pay assessment with worker representatives is required when the gap in any category is 5% or more,
  is not justified by objective gender-neutral criteria, and is not remedied within six months (Art. 10).

## Impact assessment
High impact on: recruitment playbooks, employment contract templates (confidentiality clauses), and
self-service FAQs about salary information. Owners of affected content must review before 30 September 2026.
